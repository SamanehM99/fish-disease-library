## Overview

### What is HSMI?
Heart and Skeletal Muscle Inflammation (HSMI) is a significant viral disease affecting farmed Atlantic salmon (*Salmo salar*). It is caused by the piscine orthoreovirus (PRV) and is characterised by inflammation and damage to the heart muscle, sometimes accompanied by inflammation and damage to red skeletal muscle. First detected in Norway in 1999, HSMI has since been reported in other major salmon farming regions, including Scotland, Ireland, Chile and Canada, causing substantial economic losses. It occurs most commonly during the first year at sea, although cases also occur in freshwater production  [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)[2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0011487)[6](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/hjerte-og-skjelettmuskelbetennelse-hsmb). 
HSMI has so far been documented only in farmed Atlantic salmon and is caused by the genotype PRV-1. However, related strains of PRV cause similar, but distinct viral conditions across other salmonid species. Depending on the region, host species and PRV strain, these related conditions are **HSMI-like disease** in rainbow trout (_Oncorhynchus mykiss_), **Erythrocytic Inclusion Body Syndrome (EIBS)** in coho salmon (_Oncorhynchus kisutch_), and **jaundice and anemia** (or **jaundice syndrome**) in Chinook salmon (_Oncorhynchus tshawytscha_). This disease page focuses exclusively on **HSMI** in Atlantic salmon [6](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/hjerte-og-skjelettmuskelbetennelse-hsmb)[7](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0817/9/2/143)

## Clinical Signs of Heart and Skeletal Muscle Inflammation

### Common Signs

- **Physical Signs:**
    - **Cardiac Damage:** Inflammation and damage to the heart  muscle, affecting its function. The heart may appear pale, and fluid or blood may accumulate in the pericardial cavity surrounding the heart [6](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/hjerte-og-skjelettmuskelbetennelse-hsmb).
    - **Ascites**: Fluid may accumulate in the abdominal cavity [6](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/hjerte-og-skjelettmuskelbetennelse-hsmb).
    - **Liver discolouration**: The liver may appear pale or yellowish and may show congestion associated with circulatory failure (see a reddened margin). A thin layer of fibrin may sometimes cover the liver surface [6](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/hjerte-og-skjelettmuskelbetennelse-hsmb)[8](https://akvademiet.no/fiskesykdommer/infeksjonssykdommer/hsmb-hjerte-og-skjelettmuskelbetennelse/).
    - **Enlarged spleen**: Swelling of the spleen may be observed [6](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/hjerte-og-skjelettmuskelbetennelse-hsmb).
    - **Petechial haemorrhages**: Small petechial haemorrhages may occur in the fat surrounding the internal organs [6](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/hjerte-og-skjelettmuskelbetennelse-hsmb).

- **Behavioural Changes:**
    - **Abnormal Swimming And Lethargy:** Fish may display weakened swimming due to muscle damage, where they may gather near cage walls and water surface [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)[2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0011487).
    - **Loss of Appetite:** Decreased feeding behaviour and subsequent weight loss after an outbreak [8](https://akvademiet.no/fiskesykdommer/infeksjonssykdommer/hsmb-hjerte-og-skjelettmuskelbetennelse/).
    

**Progression of Symptoms**

- **Early Stages:** Subtle signs such as reduced feed intake and slight lethargy.

- **Advanced Stages:** More pronounced physical symptoms, including muscle lesions and cardiac damage, leading to increased mortality if not managed. Morbidity may be very high, while mortalities are variable and can reach up to 20% in affected cages [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)[2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0011487).

- **Impact on Fish Health:** HSMI severely compromises heart and muscle function, making fish more susceptible to secondary infections and reducing overall vitality.

### Causes of Heart and Skeletal Muscle Inflammation

**Etiology**

- **Causative Agent:** Piscine orthoreovirus (PRV) [2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0011487)[3](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/science/aah-saa/species-especes/aq-health-sante/prv-rp-eng.html).

- **Transmission Methods:**
    - **Direct Contact:** Spread between infected and healthy fish through close proximity.
    - **Waterborne:** The virus can be transmitted through water, potentially spreading from one farm to another [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)[3](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/science/aah-saa/species-especes/aq-health-sante/prv-rp-eng.html).

**Risk Factors**

- **Environmental Factors:** Poor water quality, high stocking densities, and stressful conditions can increase susceptibility to HSMI.

- **Farm Management Practices:** Ineffective biosecurity measures and lack of routine health monitoring can facilitate the spread of PRV [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)[3](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/science/aah-saa/species-especes/aq-health-sante/prv-rp-eng.html).

### Diagnosis

**Diagnostic Methods**

- **Clinical Examination:** Observation of physical and behavioural symptoms indicative of HSMI.

- **Laboratory Tests:**
    - **PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction):** Detects PRV genetic material in fish tissues.
    - **Histopathology:** Microscopic examination of heart and muscle tissues to identify characteristic lesions of HSMI.
    - **Immunohistochemistry:** Detects viral antigens in tissue samples  [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)[3](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/science/aah-saa/species-especes/aq-health-sante/prv-rp-eng.html)[4](https://www.mdpi.com/journal/pathogens/special_issues/Heart_and_Skeletal_Muscle_Inflammation).

**Differential Diagnosis**

- **Distinguishing HSMI from Other Diseases:** It is crucial to differentiate HSMI from other diseases with similar symptoms, such as Pancreas Disease (PD) and Cardiomyopathy Syndrome (CMS)[1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)[2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0011487).

### Treatment and Prevention

**Treatment Options**

- **Current Treatments:** There are no effective antiviral treatments available for HSMI. Management focuses on preventing the spread and mitigating the impact through biosecurity measures and supportive care.

- **Experimental Treatments:** Ongoing research into potential antiviral agents and other therapeutic approaches [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)[2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0011487)[3](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/science/aah-saa/species-especes/aq-health-sante/prv-rp-eng.html).

**Preventive Measures**

- **Biosecurity Protocols:**
    - Implementing strict quarantine measures for new stock to prevent the introduction of PRV.
    - Regular disinfection of equipment and facilities to minimize the risk of contamination.

- **Farm Management Practices:**
    - Reducing stocking densities to decrease the risk of virus transmission.
    - Regular health monitoring and early detection programs to identify and manage outbreaks promptly.
    - Implementing all-in/all-out stocking practices to reduce the risk of spreading infections  [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)[3](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/science/aah-saa/species-especes/aq-health-sante/prv-rp-eng.html)[4](https://www.mdpi.com/journal/pathogens/special_issues/Heart_and_Skeletal_Muscle_Inflammation).

## Data Insights

### Disease Impact by Country

#### Norway

- **Incidence in Norway:**
    - HSMI was first diagnosed in Norway in 1999  [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)
    - There has been a yearly increase in the number of recorded outbreaks since its initial detection [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/)
    - By 2004, HSMI had been reported in 417 farms in Norway [5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/).

- **Geographical Spread:**
    - HSMI has been observed throughout Norway's salmon farming regions [2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/).
    - The disease appears to have spread along the Norwegian coastline since its initial detection.

- **Economic Impact:**
    - While specific economic data for HSMI in Norway is limited, the disease can cause significant losses due to:
        
        - High morbidity rates in affected cages [5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/).
        - Variable mortality rates, reaching up to 20% in some cases [5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/).
        - Reduced growth and feed conversion in affected fish.

- **Treatment & Management:**
    - No specific treatment exists for HSMI [2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/).
    - Management focuses on reducing environmental stress during outbreaks [2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15264718/).
    - Improved biosecurity measures and monitoring of fish health are key strategies.

## Conclusion

Heart and Skeletal Muscle Inflammation (HSMI) remains a significant threat to salmon aquaculture, necessitating diligent management and preventive measures. By implementing robust biosecurity protocols, investing in ongoing research, and fostering industry-wide cooperation, fish health managers and veterinarians can mitigate the impact of HSMI and ensure the sustainability of salmon farming operations. This comprehensive guide aims to provide the necessary information and resources to effectively manage HSMI and support the health and welfare of farmed salmon.
